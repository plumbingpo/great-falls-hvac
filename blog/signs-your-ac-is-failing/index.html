Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Great Falls, MT

Home / Blog / Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Great Falls, MT

As the hottest days approach here in Great Falls, MT, our air conditioners become hard-working systems of our home comfort. These units operate constantly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners have a lifespan and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a costly failure might be on the horizon. Catching these indicators promptly can save you from a sudden and uncomfortable breakdown on the hottest day of the year, and potentially reduce repair costs in the long run. Here at Great Falls HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: A Lack of Consistent Cooling?

One of the most noticeable and irritating signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a noticeable decrease in its cooling capability. You might find yourself adjusting the thermostat lower and lower only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Some areas of your home might be significantly hotter. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. Several factors could contribute to this issue. It could be a depleted refrigerant charge, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. The refrigerant is what makes cooling possible. Another common culprit is a clogged air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. Dirty evaporator coils can also be a factor, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. The compressor itself, the engine of your AC, could be giving out. If you're experiencing inadequate cooling, it's time to have a professional from Great Falls HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: What Are Those Unusual Sounds?

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing new and alarming sounds coming from your unit, it's a clear indicator that something is amiss. Worn motor bearings are often the source of grinding or squealing sounds. These bearings, critical for smooth operation, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Electrical issues, like a failing relay, can manifest as constant clicking. Banging or clunking noises are particularly concerning, as they might suggest a component that has come dislodged and is striking other parts. Don't delay in addressing these auditory warnings. Timely attention to these sounds can avert a minor problem from escalating into an expensive fix.

Increased Energy Bills: An Unexpected Surge in Utility Costs

A subtle but significant sign of a struggling AC is a gradual rise in your electricity costs. If your bills are increasing without a change in thermostat settings, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. Various problems can contribute to this energy drain. As mentioned, a clogged air filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. Ductwork leaks let valuable cooled air escape into areas that don't need it, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. Units that are too old or not properly sized for your home are inherently less efficient. Refrigerant leaks also force the system to work harder. The compressor and fan motors can become less efficient as they age. When your energy bills are unexpectedly high, it's worth having Great Falls HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. Our team can help you determine the most cost-effective solution.

Poor Airflow: A Trickle of Air, Not a Breeze?

Strong airflow is essential for both comfort and system efficiency. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have significantly less airflow than others, it's a major sign of an underlying problem. This problem can stem from various causes. A very common culprit is a dirty or clogged air filter, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Blocked return air vents can also hinder airflow. The fan motor, if failing, is another source of poor airflow. A diminished fan motor means less air reaching your vents. Debris or even physical damage within the ductwork can also restrict air movement. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. Fixing poor airflow is vital not only for your comfort but also to prevent overworking your AC and reducing its longevity.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Is Your AC Leaking Water?

Some condensation is expected from an AC, but significant moisture or standing water around the unit is problematic. This is a definite warning sign that something is wrong. Usually, water leaks are due to an obstructed condensate line. Humidity removed from the air collects in a drain pan and exits via a condensate line during cooling. Algae, dirt, or debris can block this line, causing water to overflow the pan. Water damage and electrical hazards are possible consequences. A damaged or corroded drain pan can also be the source of leaks. If there's a major refrigerant leak, ice can build up on the coils, melt, and cause overflow. Any standing water or excessive moisture near your AC, it's imperative to address it quickly to prevent further damage. Great Falls HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: When Your AC Can't Stay On

Normal AC operation involves consistent cycles of cooling and then resting. Rapid on-off cycles, or "short cycling," are a strong signal that your AC is in distress. Short cycling means your AC isn't completing a full cooling cycle, which is inefficient and puts undue stress on the system. There are various potential causes for this behavior. An oversized unit might short cycle because it cools the air too rapidly, then stops before it can remove humidity effectively. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. A struggling compressor or an electrical fault can also be culprits. Short cycling, regardless of its origin, quickly degrades AC parts, leading to early failure and higher bills. An AC that short cycles isn't working properly and requires professional diagnosis.

Old Age: The Inevitable March of Time for Your AC

The age of your AC unit, even if it seems fine, is a major predictor of future issues. Most central air conditioning systems are designed to last between 10 to 15 years, with some well-maintained units stretching to 20 years. An AC unit nearing or past its expected lifespan will be more prone to issues and less efficient. Older units often use outdated refrigerants like R-22, which are no longer manufactured and are becoming increasingly expensive to service due to supply limitations. The components within older systems are also simply more worn out from years of operation. While regular maintenance can certainly extend the life of your AC, there comes a point where repairs become more frequent and costly than investing in a new, more efficient system. If your AC is over a decade old and showing symptoms, it's wise to consider replacement options. A modern, efficient model can provide substantial energy savings and enhanced comfort for many years.
